Operating model · Microsoft 365 · Applied AI

Integrated S&OP operating system

Connecting operational knowledge to decisions, ownership and execution.

Problem

Silos, multiple sources of truth and fragmented decision-making created a reactive, short-term focus. Planning discussions needed clearer ownership, traceability and a connection to what happened afterwards.

People & process first

I developed the S&OP framework across people, process, data and technology. I first redesigned the process sequence and ownership, then built the tools needed to coordinate the framework at company scale. Technology supported the operating model.

The operating architecture
ERP & planning systemsMaster and transactional data, with bottom-up planning detail.
Power BI & ExcelAnalytics and quantitative insights for planning conversations.
Teams & SharePointControl tower for meetings, transcripts, attendance, calendars and embedded reporting.
Lists & Power AutomateStructured assumptions, decisions and escalations connected to action workflows.
Governed librariesKey deliverables, collaborative files and traceability across process stages.
Copilot prompts, notebooks & agentsInsight digestion, templated minutes, knowledge retrieval and communication.

Recreated architecture. No internal data or proprietary screenshots.

Decision flow

The automated escalation flow compares quantitative insights with qualitative planning assumptions and flags actions. Meeting transcripts support templated minutes and action collection. Structured records make planning information queryable, while Copilot supports synthesis and executive communication. Presentations become an output when needed rather than the place where the process lives.

Design lesson

The first Lists design captured useful qualitative context, but I missed an opportunity to distinguish numerical inputs clearly enough for analysis at scale. I released a revised model with explicit fields and identifiers aligned to keys in existing reports. That connected qualitative assumptions to the available reporting toolkit and made them easier to query. The lesson: design information around the decisions and integrations it needs to support from the outset.

What changed

The build connected reporting, discussion, decisions and follow-through in one working environment. It helped move conversations towards more meaningful questions: which meetings add value, which changes matter, and what thinking should inform the next decision. The operating framework continues to evolve through planning cycles.

Build with what is available.

I prioritised technology already available to the organisation, keeping development in-house and designing for maintainability. My entrepreneurial approach is to understand the whole problem before adding cost or complexity.

Capabilities

Operating-model design; requirements discovery; information architecture; process digitisation; workflow automation; applied AI; implementation; stakeholder enablement.

Automating a broken stocktake

Count once. Investigate the exceptions.

01Location and item
02Digital count
03Live comparison
04Exception and recount
05Controlled update

Recreated process view. No internal data or proprietary screenshots.

Problem

A manual physical inventory process tied counting, validation and reconciliation together, consuming operating time and disrupting trading.

What I designed

I redesigned the workflow around digital counting, location logic, live comparisons and exception-led recounts, using Google Sheets and scripting.

How it works

Counts entered a structured model. Differences became visible while the count was in progress, focusing attention on the items that needed investigation before system updates.

Outcome

The redesigned process brought a stocktake that previously occupied around three working days down to approximately three hours, while creating a route toward perpetual counting.

Capabilities

Process diagnosis; automation; user experience; controls; data validation

Reusable thinking

The time saving came from redesigning the work and its controls, then applying simple technology.

What I designed

How it works

Problem

L.I.L.Y. means Lean Integration Lets You. I am developing a transformation method and diagnostic approach rooted in practical operational experience.

How it works

The method examines people, process, decision rights, assumptions, data, technology, governance and measurement. Interventions progress from removal and simplification toward digitisation, automation and AI where justified.

How I think

The operating system comes first.

People should not have to be the integration

When people repeatedly carry information between reports, meetings and action logs, investigate the operating design. Connect observations to assumptions, decisions, ownership and follow-through. The aim is to release capacity for judgment and meaningful discussion.

Build, learn and improve

I identify the most critical learning path for a solution, draw on transferable skills and expert relationships, and ship an MVP that can improve as I upskill. Some of my Excel tools, SharePoint environments and templates continue to be used by colleagues after the original project.

Start with work, not software

Observe how work actually moves. Identify who makes decisions, which assumptions matter and where information breaks down. Design the operating model before selecting the technology.

Remove before you automate

Remove → Simplify → Standardise → Digitise → Automate → AI. Each step asks whether the work should exist in its current form before making it faster.

Treat assumptions as managed objects

Capture the assumption, owner, supporting evidence and expected impact. Validate against the result and retain the learning for the next cycle.

Make decisions traceable

Connect a decision to its context, owner, action, due date and escalation route. A meeting creates value when the decision changes what happens afterwards.
